1.  About this note
This note sets out our current understanding, nothing more. It gathers what we have seen and discussed so far 
into one place, so the conversation starts from a shared view. Everything here is provisional and offered for your 
steer.
2.  What we have reviewed
We have gone through the materials shared with us in detail: the clinical protocol, its full suite of working forms, 
the consent and patient information, and the implementation notes. Read through the lens of a record system, 
they show us the kind of workflow the platform must hold faithfully and provably. A few examples of what that 
means in practice:
● Multi-step supervised administration with pre-dose verification, timed post-session monitoring, and a 
structured follow-up path.
● Product custody with cold-chain handling, lot and certificate verification and reconciliation, alongside 
device logs.
● A coded, not named, subject model, adverse-event reporting on defined clocks, and a caregiver 
observation record.
The clinical judgement behind these remains entirely yours. Our concern is only that the record holds them, and can 
prove it later.
3.  What Atunse EMR is
Atunse EMR is, first and foremost, an architecture: a governed clinical record and operations platform. What runs 
on it first, and which programme becomes the flywheel, is entirely your call. The value is a record that can carry 
whatever workflow you choose, and the next one after it, without a rebuild: audit-ready, interoperable, and 
extensible. The clinical direction is yours to set; our focus is the foundation it stands on.

4.  How we see the architecture
This is how we currently picture it, offered as a starting point rather than a fixed design. A configurable protocol 
engine at the centre, wrapped by a standards-based interoperability surface, standing on a governance and 
security substrate that applies across every layer:
Atunse EMR: a high-level view, anchored to recognised health-IT standards.
A note on access: the patient is a first-class role, not only the subject of the record. Consent, sight of their own 
information, and patient-reported outcomes sit with them, with a caregiver or authorised representative alongside 
where capacity calls for it.
It is deliberately built on recognised frameworks: HL7 FHIR R4 for interoperability, IHE profiles, maturity measured 
against HIMSS EMRAM, an ISO/IEC 27001 aligned security posture, NDPA 2023 data residency, and GAMP 5 with 
ALCOA+ for validation and data integrity.

6.  Working in confidence
We are treating everything shared with us in strict confidence, to the standard we would expect under a formal 
agreement, and putting that agreement in place is a natural early step. An engagement agreement covering scope, 
deliverables and ownership would sit alongside it when the time is right. We are not proposing terms here, only 
noting the footing we are already working to.
7.  What we hope to explore
We are coming mostly to listen. These are the topics we would value working through, in whatever order and 
depth suits you:
1. Your goals, and where a record platform fits them.
2. The workflow you would want it to support first.
3. How your protocols are authored and approved.
4. The records and systems already in place.
5. Data residency, confidentiality and any sponsor data-sharing.
6. Roles, access and ownership.
7. Security and governance expectations.
8. How you would like to work together from here.
