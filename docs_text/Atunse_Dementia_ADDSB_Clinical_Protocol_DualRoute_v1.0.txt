1. Purpose and Scope
1.1 Purpose
This protocol standardizes the supervised administration of two investigational amniotic fluid–derived extracellular-vesicle (exosome) products — intranasal ExoPure™ 140B delivered via the ViaNase™ device and intravenous Rejuvenate Serum™ — to adults with dementia, and standardizes the screening, treatment, monitoring, follow-up, and documentation that surround it. Its aim is to generate structured safety, tolerability, feasibility, and exploratory clinical and biomarker data under physician supervision.
1.2 Scope
Applies to the supervising physician and trained clinical staff administering this regimen to eligible adults with dementia under appropriate institutional and regulatory authorization. It covers eligibility, screening and baseline assessment, the treatment-day workflow, intranasal and intravenous administration, post-treatment monitoring, follow-up, and required documentation. It does not constitute a claim of established efficacy and is not a replacement for standard dementia care.
1.3 Target Population
Adults with documented dementia or progressive cognitive decline for whom the supervising physician determines this investigational regimen is clinically reasonable, who can provide informed consent (directly or through a legally authorized representative), and who have a caregiver available to support monitoring and follow-up. Full eligibility criteria appear in §5.1.

5.  Procedure
5.1 Pre-Procedure Assessment (Screening and Baseline)
Complete and document before treatment. Eligibility is determined by the supervising physician.
Inclusion — all of: adult with documented dementia or progressive cognitive decline; condition serious or disabling; limited benefit from or limited access to standard care; physician determines the regimen clinically reasonable; informed consent obtainable from patient or LAR; caregiver available; able to complete baseline and follow-up assessments; able to undergo intranasal and intravenous administration.
Exclusion — any of: active systemic infection or fever; unstable cardiovascular disease; recent stroke or TIA (unless physician judges risk acceptable); recent or uncontrolled seizures; severe uncontrolled hypertension; severe renal or hepatic failure; active malignancy (unless physician-reviewed); severe coagulopathy or bleeding disorder; known hypersensitivity to product components; active nasal infection, severe obstruction, active epistaxis, recent nasal surgery, or anatomy precluding intranasal delivery; severe agitation precluding safe administration; pregnancy where biologically applicable; any condition making intravenous or intranasal administration unsafe; hormone-specific contraindications where hormone optimization is considered.
Screening steps: confirm dementia diagnosis; review history and medications (noting anticoagulants, antihypertensives, sedatives, and seizure-threshold agents); physical and neurological examination; cognitive testing (MMSE, MoCA, CDR or CDR-SB); functional assessment (ADCS-ADL or equivalent) and neuropsychiatric inventory (NPI-Q); mood screen (PHQ-9 or GDS); sleep assessment; caregiver interview; nasal eligibility examination; IV access assessment; baseline laboratory and biomarker panels; hormone panels where optimization is considered; product and device verification; risk–benefit assessment; informed consent; and confirmation of institutional and regulatory documentation.
Baseline laboratory and biomarker panel: CBC with differential; comprehensive metabolic panel; liver function tests; creatinine and eGFR; fasting glucose and HbA1c; lipid panel; urinalysis; ESR and CRP/hs-CRP; coagulation studies where indicated. Reversible-cause screen: vitamin B12, folate, TSH, free T4, vitamin D, ferritin, and magnesium. Infectious-disease screen as indicated. Inflammatory markers (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α) and neurodegenerative biomarkers (e.g., plasma p-tau, neurofilament light chain, GFAP) where available. Hormone panels per §6 where optimization is considered.
5.2 Treatment Course and Implementation Steps
Treatment course: the standardized course consists of six (6) treatment sessions administered once weekly over six consecutive weeks (Weeks 1–6). Each weekly session comprises both routes (intravenous Rejuvenate Serum followed by intranasal ExoPure 140B) on the same visit. A physician safety review precedes every session; a session is deferred or withheld if any stopping criterion (§6.3) is present.

The dose is identical at every session; there is no dose escalation. Each session uses one 1 mL vial of Rejuvenate Serum™ (70 billion EV) intravenously and one 3 mL vial of ExoPure™ 140B (140 billion EV) intranasally. The complete six-session course therefore requires 6 vials of each product (6 × Rejuvenate Serum 1 mL and 6 × ExoPure 140B 3 mL).
The steps below apply to each of the six weekly sessions.
Sequencing: intravenous Rejuvenate Serum is administered first, followed by intranasal ExoPure 140B, in the same supervised visit. Physician authorization is recorded before each administration; treatment does not proceed if any stopping criterion (§6.3) is present.
A. Before arrival — confirm authorizations on file, products in-date, Certificates of Analysis reviewed, cold chain intact, device ready, emergency kit present, and forms prepared.
B. Check-in — confirm patient identity; confirm consent current; caregiver present; review interval symptoms and medications; record baseline vital signs; focused examination.
C. Pre-dose timeout (two-person verification) — confirm correct patient, product, route, dose, device, lot and expiry, and documentation, with recorded physician authorization.
D. Intravenous administration (Rejuvenate Serum): verify identity and lot, route, dose, and diluent (the product is formulated in 0.9% saline); inspect visually for clarity and absence of aggregation; establish and confirm IV access; administer by slow IV push in 0.5 mL increments, pausing 30–60 seconds between increments (not a bolus); monitor continuously for infusion reaction; flush the line and document. The dose is one 1 mL vial (70 billion EV) administered by slow IV push over 10–15 minutes at every session, with no escalation across the course.
E. Intranasal administration (ExoPure 140B via ViaNase™): the standard intranasal dose is one 3 mL vial (1.4 × 10¹¹ EV) per session, delivered with the ViaNase™ device using the exosome-specific nose cone supplied by Kurve. Verify product and lot and the thaw-stability window, and assess the nasal passages (excluding epistaxis, significant congestion, mucosal injury, or obstruction) before starting.
Loading: using a sterile syringe, draw the full 3 mL of ExoPure 140B from the vial (introduce an equal volume of air into the vial first to offset the liquid withdrawn), then dispense the product into the device reservoir. Reattach the reservoir and nose cone to the base unit (the magnetic air-feed connection seats with one to two clicks) and confirm the device passes its performance check and holds sufficient charge to complete a full 40-second cycle. The reservoir accommodates the full 3 mL volume in a single fill; no mid-session reload is required.
Delivery: place the exosome-specific nose cone in one nostril and gently occlude the opposite nostril. The patient breathes in through the nose and out through the mouth at roughly half-effort; the device delivers only on inhalation and runs a fixed 40-second cycle, stopping automatically. Repeat one 40-second cycle in the second nostril. The full dose is defined by emptying the reservoir, not by a fixed number of activations: after the two 40-second runs, if any product remains, continue in 5-second-per-side increments (run ~5 seconds, switch, run ~5 seconds) until the reservoir is empty — typically totaling about 40–45 seconds per side.
After delivery: record the dose delivered and confirm the reservoir is empty; monitor for sneezing, nasal irritation, epistaxis, dizziness, headache, or hypersensitivity; clean and dispose of components per the Kurve instructions for use.
Regimen note: the doses above are the standardized investigational regimen for this protocol; the supervising physician confirms suitability for each patient and may adjust within the protocol’s safety framework.
5.3 Per-Session Observation and Follow-Up Schedule
After each weekly session, monitor in clinic at 15, 30, 60, and 120 minutes (or on a physician-adjusted schedule): vital signs, oxygen saturation, temperature, neurological status, nasal assessment, and infusion-reaction watch. Apply discharge criteria, then provide caregiver instructions, emergency contact, adverse-event reporting instructions, and the next-visit date.
During the 6-week course (each session): post-session in-clinic monitoring as above; pre-session physician safety review and interval-symptom/AE check at the next weekly visit; targeted safety labs and inflammatory markers as clinically indicated.

5.4 Documentation Requirements
Complete and file for each patient: eligibility and consent records; baseline assessment; laboratory and biomarker review; product accountability and cold-chain log; ViaNase™ device log; treatment administration record (lot, dose, volume, times, operator); vital-signs and monitoring sheet; AE/SAE form; hormone-decision worksheet where used; follow-up visit forms; caregiver diary; protocol-deviation form; and final case-report summary.

6. Special Considerations
6.1 Contraindications
As per the exclusion criteria in §5.1. Absolute contraindications to administration on a given day include active hypersensitivity to product components, active infection or fever, hemodynamic instability, active epistaxis or unsafe nasal anatomy (for the intranasal route), and inability to obtain valid informed consent.
6.2 Precautions
These are investigational extracellular-vesicle biologics administered by intranasal and intravenous routes under physician supervision. Maintain the cold chain and observe the post-thaw stability window. Verify product identity, lot, and visual integrity before each use, and confirm the ViaNase™ device configuration and current manufacturer instructions. Keep the emergency kit immediately available throughout administration and the observation period. Where hormone optimization is considered, complete contraindication screening before initiation.
